> [!BACKSTORY]
> Al‑Dahr was never meant to be a hero. He was meant to be a craftsman, a quiet one at that. He preferred the company of his workshop—where he could lose himself in the delicate balance of gears and glyphs—to the bustle of the village. But the Vale had a way of choosing its champions, even the unwilling ones.
> 
>  When a creature born of corrupted forest magic wandered too close to the village, panic spread. Al‑Dahr, thinking only to protect his family’s workshop, grabbed the nearest invention: a half‑tested gauntlet. He didn’t intend to fight. He intended to distract the creature long enough for others to escape. Instead, the gauntlet discharged with spectacular force, sending the beast fleeing and saving the village.
> 
> The story grew in the retelling. Soon, he was “Batal,” “Malik Al‑Dahr,” and other titles that made him cringe. He insisted it had been an accident. No one listened.
> 
> Al‑Dahr is a gentle giant with calloused hands and a giving nature. He’s humble to a fault, uncomfortable with praise, and always tinkering with some new device that may or may not explode. He avoids the spotlight, but trouble has a way of finding him—and when it does, he rises to meet it with quiet determination.
